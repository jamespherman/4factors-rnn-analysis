## 1. Two-hemisphere SC: anatomy, physiology, models, data

### Primate and cat commissural physiology (most directly relevant)
- **Munoz & Istvan 1998, J Neurophysiol 79:1193** [V], doi:10.1152/jn.1998.79.3.1193. Awake macaque, microstimulation plus recording. Saccade neurons were inhibited at short latency by stimulating the **ipsilateral (1–5 ms) or contralateral (2–7 ms)** SC, in both fixation and saccade zones. Contralateral fixation-zone stimulation excited fixation neurons at 1–2 ms. Few neurons were activated orthodromically or antidromically. *Use:* this gives the latency prior for commissural effects (a 2–7 ms lag between a contralateral burst and ipsilateral suppression), and it shows that inhibition between the two SCs exists in the monkey.
- **Takahashi, Sugiuchi, Izawa, Shinoda 2005, J Neurophysiol 94:1707** [V], doi:10.1152/jn.00347.2005 (cat, intracellular). Caudal tectoreticular (saccade) neurons received mono- or disynaptic **inhibition from the whole rostrocaudal extent of the contralateral SC**. Putative inhibitory commissural neurons had **widely branching axons** across the opposite SC. Excitatory commissural neurons were mostly rostral and terminated focally.
- **Takahashi et al. 2007, J Neurophysiol 98:2664** [V], doi:10.1152/jn.00696.2007, and **2010, J Neurophysiol 104:3146** [V], doi:10.1152/jn.00554.2010. Commissural excitation is mirror-symmetric (medial to medial, lateral to lateral, i.e. upper field to upper field). **Reciprocal inhibition links upper and lower field representations across sides.** Inhibitory commissural neurons are **small GABA+ somata (<200 µm²) in the deep layers**. Excitatory commissural neurons are medium-sized GABA– cells in the upper intermediate layer. Review: Takahashi & Shinoda 2018, Neuroscience 392:281 [V], doi:10.1016/j.neuroscience.2018.08.027.
- *Implication for the model:* commissural inhibition is **topographically structured, not uniform**. In cat it is strongly vertical-axis specific (up vs down). Commissural inhibitory neurons are projection neurons, so they are not "local-only". Some of the recorded "putative interneurons" could be **commissural I sources themselves**, and that would violate the "I units project only locally" constraint. Let the inter-collicular I→E matrix be location-specific (e.g., parameterised by the target's mirrored location). Do not use a single scalar.
- **Primate anatomy:** I could not find a primate study that identifies GABAergic tectotectal neurons by immunohistochemistry. The cat data above are the best available. **Flag: not verified for macaque.**

### Macaque behaviour and recordings of both SCs
- **Isoda & Hikosaka 2008, J Neurophysiol 100:1332** [V], doi:10.1152/jn.90275.2008. In an asymmetric-reward task, SC movement neurons burst transiently after a **small-reward target appeared opposite their movement field**, and the burst predicted RT delay. The authors interpret this as inhibition between the two SCs. *Highly relevant to our reward factor.*
- **Ratcliff et al. 2011, Neural Comput 23:1790** [V], doi:10.1162/NECO_a_00135. Simultaneous left and right SC buildup neurons (10 sessions, 26–60 pairs) in a 2AFC brightness task. **No measurable anticorrelation** between the two sides (split by high vs low target-neuron rate; CCGs flat). Same-side pairs were positively correlated.
- **Katz, Yu, Herman, Krauzlis 2023, Commun Biol 6:540** [V], doi:10.1038/s42003-023-04912-0; data on Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.12jm63z0r. *This is your own paper.* There were 6 bilateral sessions (one monkey). Spike-count correlations between the two hemispheres were **not different from zero, either positive or negative**, for any functional class. Power analysis puts the upper bound at about 0.04–0.17. Within-SC rSC depended on class, highest in Delay neurons. *Implication:* bilateral-SC data already exist in-lab. The null result on rSC between hemispheres is a strong constraint on any two-SC model. Effective coupling between the SCs must be weak, or cancel out (balanced E and I commissural input), or act only at fine timescales or specific epochs. That is a testable prediction for model A.
- **Jun et al. 2021, Nat Neurosci 24:1121** [V], doi:10.1038/s41593-021-00878-6 (Basso lab). Unilateral SC inactivation acts like a constant offset in contralateral evidence. This fits a push-pull read-out of the two SCs but does not test it directly.
- **Johnston & Smith 2026, eLife 13:RP99278** [V], doi:10.7554/eLife.99278. A slow arousal drift (pupil-linked, shared with PFC) dominates shared SC variability and is weaker in motor neurons. **Regress out slow drift before any latent analysis** (topic 4). Otherwise it becomes the first "shared drive".

### Rodent models and data
- **Kopec et al. 2015, Neuron 88:367** [V citation], doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2015.08.033. Attractor model with FOF and SC, fit to optogenetic inactivation time courses. It predicted the supralinear effect of joint inactivation. I did not verify the details of the left and right node structure.
- **Duan, Erlich, Brody 2015, Neuron 86:1491** [V], doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2015.05.042. Bilateral SC inactivation impairs Anti but not Pro.
- **Duan et al. 2021, Nat Neurosci 24:1110** [V], doi:10.1038/s41593-021-00865-x. Four-node bilateral (Pro/Anti × L/R) models. **Several circuits fit the data equally well, including some without inhibition between opposing units.** *This is a direct precedent for the identifiability problem in plan A.*
- **Essig, Hunt, Felsen 2021, Commun Biol 4:719** [V]. GABAergic SC neurons do not suppress local premotor output. The authors favour long-range inhibition.
- **Thomas et al. 2023, Nat Commun 14:7358** [V], doi:10.1038/s41467-023-43252-9 (N. Li lab). Neuropixels recordings in both hemispheres (not simultaneous), with unilateral SC photoinhibition. Recording the **opposite** SC (6 sessions) showed **contra-preferring neurons increased and ipsi-preferring neurons decreased**, i.e. antagonistic hemispheres. Activating GABAergic cells inhibits contra-preferring neurons locally and disinhibits the other side. The authors list the candidate pathways (commissural I, commissural E onto I, routes outside SC) without resolving them.
- **Takács, ..., Coen, Carandini 2026 bioRxiv** [V], doi:10.64898/2026.06.05.730072. Bilateral SC inactivation restores L/R balance and the effects are **sublinear**, suggesting weak mutual inhibition. SC carries "action pressure" more than sensory signals. Preprint; not peer reviewed.

## 2. Data-constrained RNNs with unobserved units; identifiability

- **Qian, Zavatone-Veth, Ruben, Pehlevan 2024, NeurIPS** [V], bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2024.05.24.595741. Teacher–student RNNs. With partial observation, students reproduce the recorded activity but learn a **different mechanism** (spurious line attractors, fixed points, limit cycles, especially when the teacher is non-normal). The authors recommend validating with perturbations.
- **Das & Fiete 2020, Nat Neurosci 23:1286** [V claim; page numbers M], bioRxiv 10.1101/512053. Connectivity inferred in strongly recurrent networks is biased **even with all neurons observed and unlimited data**, because correlated activity is not "explained away". The proposed remedy is to drive the network far from equilibrium with low-dimensional suppressive input. *Our analogue:* task epochs with strong, spatially specific drive (target onset, saccade burst) are the most informative.
- **Beiran & Litwin-Kumar 2025, Nat Neurosci** [V claim; authors M], doi:10.1038/s41593-025-02080-4. Connectome constraints leave dynamics degenerate. Recording a small subset removes the degeneracy and can predict unrecorded activity. This is the converse of our situation: here structural priors (mirror symmetry) constrain the unrecorded units.
- **Sourmpis, Petersen, Gerstner, Bellec 2023, NeurIPS** [V], arXiv:2306.03603 (trial matching with optimal transport; code on GitHub, linked from the paper). **Sourmpis et al. 2025/26, eLife 106827** [V]. Generic RNNs generalize poorly to held-out optogenetic perturbations. E/I structure plus spiking improves this. Gradients of the fitted model predict micro-perturbation effects.
- **Perich et al. 2020 bioRxiv, CURBD** [V], doi:10.1101/2020.12.18.423348; code at github.com/rajanlab/CURBD (MATLAB and Python). This decomposes each unit's input current by source region. *Use:* after fitting model A, split each recorded unit's input into own-SC E, own-SC I, other-SC E, other-SC I and external currents. That gives an interpretable readout of "interneurons as targets of commissural I".
- **Liu, Sacks, Golub 2025, ICML (MR-LFADS)** [V], arXiv:2506.19094. A multi-region sequential VAE that **separates communication between regions from inputs coming from unobserved regions**. On 37 synthetic datasets it beat alternatives at recovering pathways. This is a good benchmark for how other work separates "unobserved input" from "recurrent".
- **Langdon & Engel 2025, Nat Neurosci 28:665** [V], doi:10.1038/s41593-025-01869-7. Latent circuit model: a low-dimensional recurrent circuit fit jointly to activity and behaviour. It found a latent *inhibitory* suppression mechanism. This is an alternative, lower-dimensional formulation for "local vs commissural" (e.g., a latent circuit with 4 L/R × E/I nodes).

### Concrete recommendations for plan A
1. **Mirror the inputs strictly, and keep ipsilateral input weak or absent.** If each SC gets only contralateral-hemifield visual and target input, any ipsilateral-target response in a recorded unit must come through a pathway between the SCs (or an explicitly modelled shared external input). If the input layer can carry ipsilateral signals directly, the commissural pathway is unidentifiable. Fit two input variants (contra-only vs contra plus learned ipsi) and report how the commissural estimates depend on this choice.
2. **Tie the weights inside each SC, add small learned symmetry-breaking offsets with an L2 penalty, and give the two SCs independent noise.** Constrain commissural matrices with Dale's law and allow them to be topographic: weight = f(distance between the source's preferred location and the mirror of the target's). This follows the cat anatomy.
3. **Add a loss term or check for the Katz et al. 2023 null.** Single-trial correlations between the hemispheres in the model, measured between a recorded unit and its mirror twin, should be about 0. Independent noise plus trial matching makes this checkable.
4. **Identifiability protocol (teacher–student).** Build teachers that differ only in inhibitory architecture: (i) local I→E only, (ii) commissural I only, (iii) both, at Essig-like ratios of commissural to local strength (0.5, 1, 2.5, 5). Also build (iv) a teacher with **external shared input** that mimics the other SC (a confound control). Sub-sample N=30–60 "recorded" units from one side with our trial counts and Poisson noise. Fit students with ≥5 seeds each. Report: (a) a confusion matrix of the recovered architecture, (b) the error in the recovered commissural/local ratio, (c) whether held-out loss discriminates the architectures. Use lesioned students (commissural I set to 0, or local I→E set to 0) as nested model comparison.
5. **Validate with perturbations.** Following Qian et al. and Sourmpis et al., predict the effect of unilateral inactivation (muscimol data or the literature: contralateral-choice deficits, the Thomas et al. pattern in the opposite SC). Candidate architectures that fit the PSTHs equally well should diverge in these predictions.

## 3. Inferring monosynaptic connections from spike trains

- **Stark & Abeles 2009, J Neurosci Methods 179:90** [M]. Baseline from a partially hollow Gaussian convolution; Poisson test with continuity correction.
- **English et al. 2017, Neuron 96:505** [V], doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2017.09.033. About 30,000 PYR→INT pairs tested and **5.9% connected** (dense silicon probes, local pairs). Baseline: CCG convolved with a hollow Gaussian (SD 10 ms, hollow fraction 0.6). Window 0.8–2.8 ms. P_fast<0.001 plus a causal-vs-anticausal test. Against optogenetic ground truth: **TPR 81%, FPR 2%**. Ground-truth presynaptic cells needed >1000 spikes. I→E connections were not analysed (troughs are harder). Code: buzcode / CellExplorer (Buzsáki lab GitHub) [M].
- **Spivak, Levi, Sloin, Someck, Stark 2022, Commun Biol 5:520** [V], doi:10.1038/s42003-022-03450-5. Bursting distorts CCGs. **Deconvolving both ACHs** before applying the baseline predictor improves detection and the spike transmission gain (STG) estimate. Works with any baseline (tails, jitter, median, GLMCC). STG is negative for inhibition. Code: Stark lab GitHub (not confirmed in the part of the paper I read).
- **Kobayashi et al. 2019, Nat Commun 10:4468** [V], doi:10.1038/s41467-019-12225-2. GLMCC fits a GLM to the CCG with a slow baseline plus a fast synaptic kernel. It beat other methods in simulation. Code is public (GitHub/NII; check the exact repo).
- **Ren, Ito, Hafizi, Beggs, Stevenson 2020, J Neurophysiol 124** [V claim], bioRxiv 10.1101/2020.02.12.944496. Extends GLMCC with Dale's-law sign consistency and distance-dependent latency. Biggest gains for weak connections. *Fits our pipeline:* impose a fixed sign per presynaptic unit.
- **Macaque benchmark: Trepka et al. 2022, eLife 11:e79322** [V] (Moore lab, Neuropixels, V1). 25-ms jitter correction, neurons above 3 sp/s, **68,579 pairs → ~15% significant peaks, but only 136 significant troughs (≥40× fewer)**. In primate probe recordings inhibitory CCG troughs are rarely detected.

**Power (derived from the Spivak et al. formula; check it).** Excess or deficit postsynaptic spikes = STG·F_pre·T. The baseline in an ROI of width W is F_pre·F_post·T·W. So z ≈ |STG|·√(F_pre·T / (F_post·W)). Example: F_pre (interneuron) 20 sp/s, F_post 30 sp/s, T=3600 s, W=3 ms gives a minimum detectable |STG| of about 0.004 at z≈3.3. Example: F_pre 5 sp/s, T=1800 s gives about 0.011. The minimum scales as 1/√T and 1/√F_pre, and grows with √F_post. Inhibitory troughs are also limited by the floor: low-rate targets leave little room for a deep trough. Our sessions have task modulation and few, often distant, pairs. Expect **few to zero** significant I→E troughs. Only same- or neighbour-shank pairs within about 200 µm are realistic. Same-channel pairs have a censoring period of ~1 ms.

**Recommendation for plan B.** Use a jitter-corrected CCG (interval jitter of 5–25 ms preserves task-locked rate co-modulation), or convolution plus deconvolution (Spivak), followed by GLMCC/Ren-style sign-constrained fits as confirmation. Pool spikes across the whole session including the ITI. Report counts and an explicit power analysis per pair (minimum detectable STG). Use the results mainly to **validate E→I** (sharp, short-latency peaks onto putative interneurons). E→I peaks are detectable, and "putative interneurons receive local monosynaptic E input" is itself informative, because commissural targets need not. Do not expect I→E troughs. A connectivity "null" from CCGs is uninformative without the power numbers.

## 4. Latent-variable and population methods

| Method | What it gives | Data needs | Code |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPFA (Yu et al. 2009, J Neurophysiol) [M] | Smooth single-trial latents for one population | ≥~20 neurons, tens–hundreds of trials | MATLAB (Yu lab); Elephant (Python) |
| Factor analysis / shared variance (Williamson et al., Umakantha et al.) [M] | Shared vs private variance per neuron; loading on each factor | ~100+ trials | sklearn FactorAnalysis |
| Population coupling (Okun et al. 2015, Nature 521:511) [M] | Each neuron's coupling to population rate; "choristers vs soloists" | Very data-efficient | Simple to implement |
| Reduced-rank regression / communication subspace (Semedo et al. 2019, Neuron 102:249) [M] | Predictive dimensions between source and target populations, after removing PSTHs | Tens of neurons per side, hundreds of trials | github.com/joao-semedo/communication-subspace [M] |
| DLAG (Gokcen et al. 2022, Nat Comput Sci 2:512) [V], doi:10.1038/s43588-022-00282-5 | Across-area latents with **signed time delays** (direction), plus within-area latents | Validated on V1–V2 (tens of units per area) | MATLAB, inside github.com/egokcen/mDLAG [V] |
| mDLAG (Gokcen et al. 2023, NeurIPS 36) [V] | ≥3 groups; ARD sets the dimensionality automatically; Gaussian observations | Same as DLAG | github.com/egokcen/mDLAG [V] |
| TAME-GP (Balzani et al. 2023, ICLR) [V], arXiv:2209.02816 | Poisson; latents split into within/across-area and task-relevant/irrelevant parts; works without trial repeats | Moderate | Savin lab GitHub [M] |
| MR-LFADS (Liu, Sacks, Golub 2025, ICML) [V] | Communication + unobserved input + local dynamics | Large datasets | Golub lab [M] |
| dPCA (Kobak et al. 2016, eLife) [M] | Splits PSTH variance by task variable (location × factor × time) | PSTHs | github.com/machenslab/dPCA |

### Unilateral recordings: does a subset share a latent drive with the "mirror" signals?
We have no single-trial access to the other SC, so the test has two levels.

1. **Condition-averaged (mirror template) regression.** Build two predictor sets from classic neurons: own-SC template = classic PSTHs on condition c, and mirror template = classic PSTHs on the mirrored condition c' (the location reflected across the vertical meridian, same factor levels). The mirror template is an estimate of the opposite SC's population activity under the symmetry assumption. Regress each putative interneuron's PSTH on the two sets with cross-validated group-lasso or reduced-rank regression, including lags of 0–10 ms. Measure the unique variance explained by each set. Commissural-target interneurons should load on the mirror template with **negative sign, at a lag of 2–7 ms** (Munoz & Istvan). Local-inhibition sources should load positively on the own-SC template with no lag. Shuffle controls: permute location labels, keeping factors fixed. Note that dPCA cannot do this directly; this is a custom regression.
2. **Single-trial residuals (noise correlations).** Katz et al. 2023 showed that shared input to the two hemispheres is roughly independent. So neurons whose trial-to-trial variance comes mainly from the other SC should have **low rSC and low factor-analysis shared variance with own-SC classic neurons**, specifically on ipsilateral-target trials, compared with neurons driven locally. Run FA or GPFA on the classic neurons (after regressing out slow drift; Johnston & Smith 2026). Then ask how much of each interneuron's residual is predicted by those own-SC latents (cross-validated R² vs population coupling), split by target hemifield. The prediction under "other-SC drive" is a dissociation: modulated by ipsilateral targets, but uncoupled from own-SC fluctuations on those trials. An alternative is a TAME-GP or pCCA model with an "own" latent block plus a residual block.
3. **Cross-check with model A.** Apply the same two analyses to synthetic teacher data. That tells you whether they discriminate the architectures.

### Bilateral recordings (few neurons per side)
- Start with **pairwise rSC and jitter-corrected CCGs between hemispheres**, using the Katz et al. framework and bootstrapped power bounds. These are the most data-efficient measures and directly comparable to prior work.
- Then use **reduced-rank regression / CCA** (Semedo), cross-validated, after subtracting PSTHs, to get the dimensionality and the strength of communication.
- For directionality, use **DLAG**. It is designed for two populations, works with tens of neurons, and returns signed delays. It assumes Gaussian observations, so use square-root spike counts in 10–20 ms bins. Delays between the SCs (~2–7 ms) are below typical bin widths. Use DLAG for which side leads at the latent level and CCGs for synaptic timing.
- mDLAG's ARD is useful when trials are limited because it selects dimensionality automatically. MR-LFADS is probably too data-hungry for a couple of sessions.
- Report findings for each epoch (visual, delay, saccade) and condition (target contra vs ipsi to each probe). Push-pull coupling may appear only around target onset or saccade, while whole-trial rSC stays near 0.

## 5. Cell classification using correlation structure as well as waveform

- **Primate SC:** I found **no** study that identifies macaque SC interneurons by waveform or by CCG. Massot, Jagadisan, Gandhi 2019 (Commun Biol 2:287, doi:10.1038/s42003-019-0527-y) [V] did laminar profiling with MUA/LFP and no cell typing. **Flag: there appears to be a gap in the literature, which would be an opportunity.**
- **Rodent SC:** Sooksawate et al. 2011, Eur J Neurosci [V citation] (GAD67-GFP; intralaminar, interlaminar and commissural GABAergic classes with distinct properties). The commissural class is a distinct GABAergic projection type, which bears on whether our "I" labels are local. Mouse SC extracellular waveform clustering in Fragile X: Open Biol 2025, doi:10.1098/rsob.250269 [V], about half of SU in a narrow-spike-like cluster. Functional cell types in mouse SC: Li & Meister 2023 eLife 12:e82367 [V title].
- **Cortex (methods to borrow):** Barthó et al. 2004, J Neurophysiol [M] used monosynaptic CCG signs to validate waveform classes. In CellExplorer (Petersen et al. 2021, Neuron) [M], putative E/I tags come from CCG peaks and troughs. WaveMAP (Lee et al. 2021, eLife 10:e67490) [V URL] does UMAP plus Louvain clustering of macaque PMd waveforms. Trainito et al. 2019, Curr Biol [M]: four waveform classes in primate cortex. Caveat: narrow spikes are not diagnostic in primates (thin-spike pyramidal tract neurons in macaque M1; Vigneswaran et al. 2011, J Neurosci 31:14235 [V URL]).
- **Recommendation:** add to the functional labels (i) waveform features (trough-to-peak, repolarisation; WaveMAP-style clustering), (ii) population coupling and FA shared variance with classic neurons, and (iii) CCG-validated E→I input where power allows. Test whether "putative interneurons" split into a **locally coupled** group (high coupling with own-SC classic neurons; receives E→I peaks) and a **decoupled, ipsi-suppressed** group (a candidate commissural target or source). Treat the split as a hypothesis to test on held-out sessions, not as a definition.
