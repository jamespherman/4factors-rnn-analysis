Aim 1 preliminary data (draft, ~330 words; figure panels in results/aim1_panels/R01_core/)

Preliminary data: SC receives goal signals already combined, and the two colliculi share slow state rather than resolving competition through fast local interactions.

In single-target recordings from SC in two monkeys (reward cue, location probability, salience and image identity varied independently), the reward cue and the location prior interacted in behaviour. With a high-reward cue, saccades to the expected location were faster than to the opposite location (10%); with a low-reward cue the reverse held (8%; monkey F; monkey N showed only the high-reward cost). Classic SC neurons showed the same crossover in their visual responses (Fig. Xa; P1b): with the high-reward cue an expected target evoked a larger response than an unexpected one (+1.0 sp/s), and with the low-reward cue a smaller one (-1.9 sp/s; reward x expectation interaction +0.27 SD, p = 7x10^-6, n = 50 neurons). Across neurons, stimulus-driven effects were aligned (image vs salience, r = 0.40, p = 4x10^-10, n = 226), but goal-directed effects were not aligned with them (location prior vs image, r = -0.05, n = 140; Fig. Xb; P2), arguing against a single priority dimension computed within SC. Competition was nonetheless expressed across the SC map: pre-saccadic suppression was most common when the target appeared opposite a neuron's response field (33% of putative interneurons vs 19% at 90 deg, p = 0.0015; classic neurons 12.5% vs 3%, p = 0.039; Fig. Xc; P3). In pilot bilateral recordings during a two-target choice task, the pre-target difference between left and right SC activity predicted which target the monkey chose (z = 3.78 and 2.32 in two sessions, against circular-shift nulls, beyond trial history, reward and gaze; Fig. Xd; P4a), pre-target activity co-fluctuated across the colliculi (partial r = 0.16, p = 0.011), and cross-collicular spike correlations showed no fast coupling at commissural latencies (2-8 ms bands within 0.9% of chance; Fig. Xe; P4b). Together, these data are consistent with SC executing a competition whose goal-directed weighting arrives already combined and is biased by slow, bilaterally shared input, as a hub predicts, rather than being resolved by fast local interactions within and between the colliculi. Aim 1 tests this directly.
